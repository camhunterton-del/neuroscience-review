Find your blind spot

Every eye has a spot where it truly cannot see. It sits where the optic nerve leaves the retina, so there are no light-sensing cells there and nothing that lands on it ever registers. This test finds yours in about a minute. Cover one eye, lock the other on the cross, and slide the dot outward until it drops into the gap your brain normally hides from you.

The science of your blind spot

The blind spot is the optic disc, the roughly 1.5 millimetre patch at the back of each eye where about a million nerve fibers gather and leave as the optic nerve. Because that patch carries wiring instead of photoreceptors, any image that falls on it is simply not recorded. You have one in each eye, off to the outer side of center, and you go through life never noticing either of them.

The reason you never notice is that the brain quietly completes the missing patch with whatever surrounds it, an effect called filling-in. In the test above you can watch it happen. Slide a plain dot into the gap and it disappears, but replace it with a continuous line or a speckled field and the brain paints the texture straight across the hole, so the gap looks seamless even though the eye sent nothing. The French physicist Edme Mariotte first described the blind spot in 1668, and the filling-in that hides it has been studied carefully since.
