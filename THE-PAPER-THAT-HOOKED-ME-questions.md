# The Paper That Hooked Me

A short, recurring Q&A column in The Neuroscience Review. A researcher or student answers five set questions about one paper that shaped how they think. It is the easiest way to earn a first byline here. You answer the prompts in a few sentences each, and we handle the formatting, the sourcing, and the figure.

## How it works

Pick one paper that genuinely changed how you see your field. Answer the five questions below in plain language, the way you would explain it to a smart friend who is not a scientist. Aim for two to five sentences per answer. Send your answers and the full citation to ckh2127@columbia.edu with the subject line "Hooked."

## The five questions

1. **What is the paper, and who did it?** Give the title, the authors, the year, and one sentence on what they set out to find.

2. **What did they actually do, and what did they find?** Walk through it the way you would for a friend, and define any jargon the first time you use it.

3. **Why did this one hook you?** What made it stick with you when so many papers do not.

4. **What does it change about how you think, or how the field thinks?** Be honest about how big or small that shift really is.

5. **What does it not prove?** Name the limits, the open questions, and anything people tend to read into it that the data does not actually support.
